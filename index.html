Hallym University / School of Software

Information Theory and
Statistical Inference Lab

We study the mathematical foundations of communication, learning, and inference. Our work asks how much information is needed—and how it should be represented—to communicate reliably, learn from data, and make decisions under uncertainty.

Research

  1. 01

    Information theory and communication

    Coding strategies and fundamental limits for multiuser networks, joint source–channel communication, and distributed information processing.

  2. 02

    Compression and generative learning

    Rate–distortion–perception tradeoffs, diffusion-based compression, and semantic representations that preserve the information relevant to a task.

  3. 03

    Generative watermarking

    Reliable information embedding through pretrained generators, with distribution preservation, secret-key constraints, and robustness to regeneration.

  4. 04

    Statistical inference and sensing

    Quickest change detection and integrated sensing and communication: understanding the tradeoffs between data rate, detection delay, and error probabilities.
